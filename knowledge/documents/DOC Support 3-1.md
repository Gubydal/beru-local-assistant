Edward Hall (1914-2009, USA) 
Discipline : Anthropology 
 
Working on reservations in the Southwest of the USA, he witnessed the clash of four different 
cultures. Among theses cultures : 
.Navajos, with their strict forms of respect. 
.Hopi, with people’s frugal and religiously faithful. 
 
He developed a cultural model focusing on non-verbal signals which pioneered the stydy of non-
verbal communication and interactions between different ethnicla groups. 
He identified two keys elements of cultural differences by describing Low and High context (also 
called Rich and Poor context). 
He studied the distance ans his use of space as being a function of culture, status, and personality 
(Proxemics). 
 
 
Claude Levi-Strauss (1908-2009, F) 
Discipline : Anthropology and Philosophy - Founder of modern structuralism 
 
He demonstrated that the comparison of cultures was biased by old stereotypes. 
For instance, he explained that races had nothing to do with civilization but only with geographical 
location, hence discarding the notion of racism. 
 
 
Franz Boas (1858-1942, D-USA) 
Discipline : Anthropology - Called the father of American anthropology 
 
He proposed a quite radically definition of culture : it is a set of beliefs, customs and social 
institutions that characterize and individualize different societies. Americain anthropolgy heritated 
his approach.

Ruth Benedict (1887-1948, USA) 
Discipline : Anthropology 
 
Her works, dealing with the differences in temperament and national character of various groups, 
discuss the cultural implications of different nations.  
She studied the relationship between culture and racial equality. 
 
On on her books on japanees culture (1946) is still considered compulsory reading for students of 
japanese culture. 
 
 
Margaret Mead (1901-1978, USA) 
Discipline : Anthropology 
 
Most of her works focused on sex roles, child development and education and their impact on 
culture and personality. 
She pioneered works in the field of gender consciousness and also she is considered as an early 
researcher on feminism. 
 
 
Konrad Lorenz (1903-1989, Aus) & Nikolaas Tinbergen (1907-1988, D) 
Discipline : Ethology (study of animal behavior) 
 
They researched the differences between instinctive and learned behaviors and they theorized the 
concept of « imprinting » 
Their studies are great help when studying human behavior, in particular cultural cues and rituals. 
 
 
Edward Sapir (1884-1939, USA) 
Discipline : Anthropology - Linguistic 
 
He demonstrated that language is not only a study of culture and words but mainly a reflection of 
relations and influence. 
He thinks that semiotics (as a study of signs’ meaning) is also useful to illustrate cultural 
differences.  
For instance, color coding is highly related to culture : In general, in the West, white is associated 
with a positive connotation (birth, wedding), but in the Chinese, Japanese, and Indian traditions, 
white is the color of grief, death and ghosts.

Ferdinand de Saussure (1857-1913, SWI) 
Discipline : Linguistics 
 
He pionnered linguistic innovative studies.  
His research still exerts a considerable influence on various disciplines, in particular on linguistics, 
literary theory, philosophy, and the social sciences. 
His contribution is useful for understanding cultures, through the language which is a very visible 
component. 
 
 
Max Weber (1864-1920, D) 
Discipline : Sociology 
 
In his book, The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism, he proposes that religion is one of 
the reasons Western cultures developed in distinct ways.  
In particular, he underlines the importance ascetic Protestantism in the development of 
capitalism.  
 
 
Emile Durkheim (1858-1917, F) 
Discipline : Sociology 
 
He has provided very useful insights for understanding societies’ functioning aspects and their 
cultures. 
He studied issues such as the division of social work (notion of solidarity), the basic forms of 
religious life, etc.
